# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

One audience decides whether this site works: hiring managers, founders, and tech leads at small crypto and DeFi startups evaluating Adriel Oloko for blockchain engineer roles. They arrive from an outreach message, a GitHub link, or a resume. They scan for protocol depth, shipped on-chain work, and evidence that this person figures out undocumented behavior and ships. They are risk-averse by default because they hire people to move money. The site must answer, within one pass, "can this person handle our contracts, our stack, and our timeline?"

A second, smaller audience: founders and teams who need a Web3-capable builder for a launchpad, dapp, or Web3 landing page. They want the same proof plus a quick path to a conversation.

## Product Purpose

Adriel Oloko is an independent blockchain and full-stack engineer. He has built and shipped Web3 launchpads as an independent contractor, integrated live protocols (Flap.sh, Uniswap Liquidity Launchpad / pools.trade on Robinhood Chain, PancakeSwap), and produced verified on-chain work: version-skew detection, feature-disabled launch path discovery, RPC log-limit workarounds, and verification tooling green against live RPCs.

This site is his front door for blockchain engineering roles and contracts. It is a website version of a very expressive "Who is Adriel Oloko", and the design itself must demonstrate the full-stack craft being pitched. Success looks like this: a hiring manager at a small crypto startup reads the site and concludes "this person finds real bugs in real protocols and ships", then replies to the outreach. The site is the proof-of-work for the pitch, not a brochure.

## Brand Personality

Voice: first-person, direct, confident, no fluff. Technical without being cold, premium without being corporate-bland.

Three words: deep, shipped, expressive.

Emotional goals: trust ("he can handle money-moving code"), delight (the site itself is living proof of craft), and a real sense of personality and care. The visitor should feel an authored point of view from someone who has actually deployed on-chain, not a template.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS template: Inter-everything, centered hero, three identical feature cards, blue gradient CTA.
- Agency cliché: stock hero photos, "we are a full-service digital agency" filler, jargon-laden copy.
- Portfolio cliché: a grid of identical screenshot cards, generic gallery feel, no point of view, no depth.
- Crypto-bro cliché: hype words, moon graphics, gradient everything, unearned protocol jargon, fake "blockchain expert" badges.
- Plain and boring: safe, forgettable, visually quiet. This site must be expressive, opinionated, and hand-made.

## Design Principles

1. The site is the proof. Every typographic, motion, and layout decision demonstrates the full-stack craft being pitched. Practice what you preach.
2. Depth over decoration. Blockchain credibility comes from specifics: live protocol names, real integrations, verifiable findings. The design frames the engineering; it never substitutes for it.
3. Expressive and authored. Avoid every template reflex. The site should feel like a person made it, because that is the pitch.
4. One-audience clarity. Every section serves the crypto hiring decision first: proof of protocol depth for the engineer, proof of full-stack craft for the founder. No section exists to flatter an agency image.
5. No fluff, direct value. Clear skills, defined capabilities, honest signals about what work is public and what is client work. Every word earns its place; outcomes beat jargon.
6. Money-risk honesty. Client work is owned plainly ("independent contractor, Web3 token launchpad for a private client, unnamed by choice"). Never invent an NDA; never describe client operational tooling in public artifacts.
7. Warm confidence. Premium quality without cold corporate distance. The "Who is Adriel Oloko" personality shows through in the details.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA is the working target. Semantic landmarks, full keyboard navigability, visible focus states, sufficient contrast (including text over imagery), reduced-motion support for all animation, and accessible labels on the contact form.
